# India Plastic Waste Management Rules — validated checkpoint table

**Review date:** 7 September 2026  
**Regulatory baseline:** Plastic Waste Management Rules, 2016, as amended through the Plastic Waste Management (Amendment) Rules, 2026, G.S.R. 237(E), 31 March 2026.

> **Confidence convention:** **H** means the rewritten value below is directly supported by the cited Gazette provision and, where useful, corroborated by an official CPCB or Government of India source. It does not mean the original submitted wording was correct.

## Executive conclusion

All seven checkpoints can be raised to **H**, but only after correcting three substantive issues:

1. **G.S.R. 237(E) is not the correct anchor for every row.** It directly matters to the recycled-content obligation and recycled-plastic marking, but the registration/category framework comes principally from G.S.R. 133(E) and G.S.R. 201(E), while thickness and the listed SUP prohibition come from Rule 4 and G.S.R. 571(E).
2. **There are five current packaging categories, not four.** G.S.R. 201(E) added Category V for biodegradable-plastic packaging in March 2024.
3. **A QR/barcode is not mandatory on every plastic package.** From 1 July 2025, Rule 11(1A) allows a producer, importer or brand owner to provide the Rule 11(1) information through one of three alternative routes: barcode/QR code, product-information brochure, or a qualifying unique number printed on the package. The selected route must be notified to CPCB.

## Row-by-row reasoning

### 1. Registration

The concept is right, but the legal chain should be cleaner:

- Rule 9(1) sends the EPR obligation to Schedule II.
- Schedule II paragraph 6 is the specific registration provision.
- Paragraph 6.1 identifies who registers; paragraph 6.2 bars business without registration; paragraph 6.3 bars dealing with an entity required to register but not registered.
- Paragraph 10.1 repeats the portal-registration obligation for the relevant entity classes.

This row should therefore cite **Schedule II paragraphs 6.1–6.5 and 10.1**, not generically cite the 2026 amendment. The 2026 instrument does not amend paragraph 6.

### 2. Packaging categories

The proposed classification accurately described Categories I–III but was no longer complete after 14 March 2024. G.S.R. 201(E):

- expanded Category IV to include compostable-plastic **commodities**, not only sheets/carry bags; and
- added Category V for biodegradable-plastic packaging, carry bags and commodities.

Do not merge Categories IV and V. “Compostable” and “biodegradable” have distinct statutory treatment and marking requirements.

### 3. Recycled-content minimums

The Gazette table is unambiguous:

| Category | FY 2025–26 | FY 2026–27 | FY 2027–28 | FY 2028–29 onward |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| I | 30% | **40%** | 50% | 60% |
| II | 10% | 10% | 20% | 20% |
| III | 5% | 5% | 10% | 10% |

The same numerical trajectory was present in the 2022 Schedule II. The 2026 amendment re-substituted the relevant producer, importer and brand-owner clauses and added or clarified the statutory-prohibition exception, proof through annual returns, audit/verification direction, Category III plastic-layer basis, and the limited FY 2025–26 food-contact carry-forward.

The importer rule is commercially important: recycled material already incorporated into imported packaging is not credited toward the importer’s use-of-recycled-content obligation under the amended clause. That is a certificate-purchase obligation, not an instruction to recalculate the imported packaging’s physical percentage.

### 4. EPR target versus recycling minimum

Keep these as two different controls:

1. The EPR target is applied to the eligible-quantity formula—25%, 70%, then 100%.
2. The minimum recycling percentage is applied to the resulting EPR target. Only the non-recyclable balance can proceed to permitted end-of-life routes under the applicable rules and guidance.

Calling the first table simply a “collection rate” loses the statutory denominator and can lead to a faulty compliance calculation.

### 5. Thickness

The proposed numeric thresholds are correct, but the instruments were mixed together. G.S.R. 571(E) itself created the 75 μm and 120 μm staged carry-bag thresholds. G.S.R. 522(E) is a real amendment, dated **6 July 2022** and published/effective **7 July 2022**, but it is not the source of those thresholds.

The 50 μm sheet requirement should retain its qualification: it concerns non-integral plastic sheet/cover used for packaging or wrapping, and Rule 4(1)(d) includes the functionality exception.

### 6. Listed single-use plastics

The law prohibits six activities—manufacture, import, stocking, distribution, sale and use—and applies to a closed list. Avoid “etc.” in a compliance register because it makes the scope appear illustrative. Also retain the precise “less than 100 micron” qualification for plastic/PVC banners.

### 7. Marking and labelling

Three legal layers need to remain separate:

- **Rule 11(1):** the underlying identification/thickness information and its exceptions.
- **Rule 11(1A):** optional media for supplying the Rule 11(1) information from 1 July 2025; QR/barcode is only one of the alternatives.
- **Rule 11(2):** the separate mandatory recycled-plastic conformity, label and marking rule under IS 14534:2023, plus applicable FSSAI labelling for food-contact use.

No reliable basis was found for a universal QR payload containing “origin, recycled percentage and EPR registration number.” The Gazette instead incorporates the information specified in Rule 11(1). The recycled percentage/mark is governed separately by Rule 11(2).

## Recommended register wording

Use the **Validated value** column above as the operative rule text. Replace the original heading with:

> **India — Plastic Waste Management Rules, 2016, consolidated through G.S.R. 237(E), 31 March 2026**

This avoids implying that every checkpoint was created or amended by G.S.R. 237(E).

## Legal-use note

This is a provision-level regulatory validation, not a product-specific legal opinion. Product composition, food-contact use, pharmaceutical/agrochemical controls, state or local plastic restrictions, and the entity’s exact producer/importer/brand-owner role can add obligations or exceptions beyond this table.
